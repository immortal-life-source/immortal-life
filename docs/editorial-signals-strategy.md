# Immortal.life Signals editorial strategy

Approved 3 October 2026.

## Product decision

Immortal.life Signals is the publication layer built from the connected longevity index. It is not a generic lifestyle blog and it must not manufacture commentary merely to increase publishing frequency.

The public audience creates discovery and trust. Professional monitoring can create recurring revenue. Institutional intelligence can create enterprise value.

## Readers

Signals should serve several distinct reader jobs without pretending they are the same audience:

- The science-curious public asks what is real and how advanced it is.
- Healthy-ageing searchers need evidence context without personal protocols or medical recommendations.
- Researchers and analysts need to see what changed, inspect the cohort, and open the sources.
- Biotechnology companies, funders and investors need field movement, organisations and evidence gaps—not investment advice.
- Journalists and creators need a defensible question, a reusable visual, a dated number and a permanent source route.
- Universities and research organisations need transparent activity context rather than quality rankings.

## Editorial contract

Every Signal must:

1. Start with a precise question.
2. Give a short descriptive answer.
3. Show one meaningful visual.
4. State the cohort and snapshot date.
5. Link important numbers to their records or filtered views.
6. Explain what the data means and what it cannot establish.
7. Keep laboratory, animal, human, trial-registration and reported-result evidence distinct.
8. Preserve the difference between official direct grants and publication funding acknowledgements.
9. Provide a corrections route and a suggested citation.

Signals must not:

- Give personal medical advice, protocols, doses or treatment recommendations.
- Infer effectiveness, safety or scientific quality from record volume.
- Describe indexed activity as a ranking of institutional quality.
- Treat a trial registration as a result.
- Treat planned enrolment as completed participation.
- Treat absent registry results as proof of misconduct or proof that no results exist elsewhere.
- Mix restricted-source data into paid delivery or exports.
- Hide incomplete coverage behind a large headline number.

## Publishing sequence

The first three stories demonstrate the mature parts of the current index:

1. The global map of active longevity trial registrations.
2. The trial-results visibility gap.
3. The connected longevity funding landscape.

Research-momentum, university-leadership, cross-topic and funding-versus-human-evidence stories must wait until their underlying reindex or historical coverage is complete enough for the exact claim being made. This is separate from the future all-at-once publication of maintained interpretations across all 180 Living Evidence Dossiers.

## Automatic publication

Signals creates a bounded weekly edition automatically and refreshes its facts every day. The automatic layer uses controlled topic-pulse templates; it does not ask a language model to invent headlines, explanations or conclusions.

An automatic topic Signal qualifies only when the latest six-week window contains at least eight public-display-approved source events, includes multiple record types or an important event, and includes at least one trial, regulatory or integrity event. At most six topics are selected for a weekly edition. Every page names the rule, shows the previous six-week window, links its source events, and describes movement in the immortal.life index—not scientific progress in the outside world.

Automatic pages must fail closed when their stored cohort or source events are unavailable. Historical ingestion and taxonomy backfill must never be described as a research breakthrough, evidence of effectiveness, or a real-world increase in scientific activity.

## Product placement

Signals belongs within the existing Briefings journey and uses `/signals` as its concise public publication address. It should be discoverable from Briefings, News, You, sitemaps and related journeys without adding another crowded top-level desktop navigation item.

## Commercial ladder

- Free: Signals, public maps, source links, feeds and topic-change views.
- Professional: monitored topics and meaningful-change briefings.
- Institutional: defined field questions, baseline landscapes, alerts and source-linked exports.
- Data: permitted datasets, APIs and embeddable views, always subject to source rights.

## Success measures

Measure privacy-safe aggregate use by page type: Signal views, source opens, filtered-view opens, dataset downloads, briefing subscriptions, topic follows and institutional enquiries. A Signal succeeds when readers verify or reuse the evidence—not merely when a page receives impressions.
