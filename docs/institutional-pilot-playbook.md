# Immortal.life Institutional Pilot Playbook

## Objective

Win one paid founding pilot with a longevity biotechnology company, laboratory, investment team, research institute, or strategic innovation group. The pilot proves that Immortal.life can turn a defined intelligence question into a maintained, source-linked decision-support workflow.

The first pilot is not intended to maximize revenue. It is intended to produce:

- one paying institutional reference;
- evidence of repeated weekly use;
- a measurable before-and-after account of the customer's information workflow;
- a testimonial or anonymized case study, subject to permission;
- a product specification for a recurring institutional subscription.

## The offer

**Name:** Immortal.life Intelligence Pilot  
**Duration:** six weeks  
**Founding price:** €2,500–€5,000, depending on the number of monitored questions, delivery format, and reporting depth  
**Scope:** five to ten topics, interventions, companies, competitors, or research questions  
**Delivery:** baseline landscape, five weekly change briefs, source-linked alerts where warranted, and a closing decision report

The pilot is a research-intelligence and discovery service. It is not medical advice, a treatment recommendation, investment advice, a formal systematic review, or a substitute for scientific, regulatory, legal, or commercial diligence.

## Ideal first customer

Prioritize organisations that meet at least four of these conditions:

1. They already make decisions in longevity, geroscience, biotechnology, preventive medicine, or age-related disease.
2. Missing a new trial, competitor programme, safety notice, correction, or research-group movement would have a real cost.
3. They do not have a large internal competitive-intelligence team.
4. They can name a narrow question that matters during the next 90 days.
5. A director, founder, principal, head of research, or business-development leader can sponsor a small pilot directly.
6. They are willing to evaluate usefulness weekly and participate in a closing review.

Avoid beginning with organisations that want personal medical recommendations, unrestricted raw-data redistribution, a claim of complete global coverage, custom consulting outside the pilot, or a formal systematic review.

## Example customer and concrete pilot

**Customer:** a small biotechnology company developing a senolytic programme.  
**Decision:** where is the field moving, which competing mechanisms are advancing, and which trial or safety changes deserve management attention?  
**Monitored set:** senolytics, cellular senescence, navitoclax, dasatinib and quercetin, FOXO4, BCL-2 family targets, fibrosis, osteoarthritis, frailty, and relevant competing companies.

### Week 1 baseline

- Map matched human, preclinical, and synthesis records.
- List registered trials by status, phase, indication, intervention, sponsor, and geography.
- Identify active universities and research groups from indexed affiliations.
- State regulatory, correction, retraction, and source-coverage context.
- Identify explicit gaps: missing results, early-stage evidence, unvalidated biomarkers, or absent safety context.

### Weekly brief example

The brief does not dump every new paper. It might say:

> Two new records and one trial update materially affected the monitored landscape this week. A registered study changed recruitment status in the source registry; a human observational paper expanded evidence in a monitored indication; and a correction affected a previously indexed mechanistic paper. No new posted trial results or relevant official safety notice were found in the commercially approved monitored sources. Open the three source records below.

Each statement links to the underlying source record and explains why it was included, what it can support, and what it cannot support.

### Closing report

- What changed during six weeks.
- Which assumptions became stronger, weaker, or remained unresolved.
- Which competitors, institutions, interventions, and trial programmes merit continued monitoring.
- Where source coverage was strong, limited, delayed, or directory-only.
- Whether a continued monthly or weekly service would save time or improve decision awareness.

## Six-week operating plan

### Week 0 — qualify and define

- Conduct one 30-minute discovery call.
- Capture the decision, monitored questions, excluded topics, jurisdictions, delivery recipients, and expected use.
- Agree that consequential conclusions remain the customer's responsibility.
- Produce a one-page scope, source-coverage statement, price, and acceptance criteria.

### Week 1 — baseline

- Deliver the initial evidence, trial, university, integrity, and regulatory landscape.
- Record the current state so later changes can be measured.
- Ask the customer to mark useful, irrelevant, missing, and already-known items.

### Weeks 2–5 — monitor and learn

- Deliver one brief on a fixed weekday.
- Send an out-of-cycle alert only for a previously defined high-importance event.
- Track which links are opened and which items are marked useful, subject to the agreed privacy terms.
- Refine matching without changing the agreed scope silently.

### Week 6 — close and convert

- Deliver the closing report.
- Hold a 45-minute review.
- Quantify useful signals, noise removed, time saved, missed-source gaps, and requested additions.
- Offer a continuing subscription only when the customer confirms recurring value.

## Commercial and source controls

1. Paid deliverables may query and reproduce only records whose source is explicitly approved for paid distribution under the source-rights policy.
2. Restricted or unclear sources may appear as named directory entries or outbound links when permitted, but their data must not be copied into paid reports.
3. Directory coverage must never be described as live ingestion.
4. Every report must state monitored sources, time window, topic scope, known delays, and material exclusions.
5. Every material item should retain a direct source link.
6. Trial registration must not be presented as a result, proof of efficacy, or proof of safety.
7. University activity must not be presented as teaching quality, research quality, or a recommendation.
8. Automated counts must not be presented as scientific consensus.
9. Do not provide diagnosis, dosing, prescribing, or individualized treatment guidance.
10. Do not call the work a systematic review unless a documented systematic-review protocol is actually followed.

## Discovery-call questions

1. What decision do you expect to make during the next 90 days?
2. Which five to ten topics, programmes, companies, or competitors affect that decision?
3. What would be costly to learn late?
4. Which sources and internal tools do you monitor today?
5. How much staff time does that take each week?
6. Which events deserve an immediate alert, and which belong in a weekly summary?
7. Which jurisdictions and languages matter?
8. Who will read the output and what will they do with it?
9. What would make the pilot clearly successful or clearly unsuccessful?
10. Are there confidentiality, data-processing, procurement, or redistribution constraints?

## Success measures

- At least one recipient opens or discusses each weekly brief.
- At least five items across the pilot are marked decision-relevant or genuinely new.
- The customer reports a defensible reduction in manual monitoring time.
- Fewer than 20% of included items are marked irrelevant after the first refinement week.
- Source and coverage limitations are understood rather than mistaken for complete global surveillance.
- The customer requests continued monitoring, an expanded scope, or an internal introduction.

## Outreach sequence — prepare, then obtain approval before sending

No message should be sent until the user selects the recipient and expressly approves outreach.

### Short introduction

Subject: A six-week longevity intelligence pilot for [organisation]

> I am building Immortal.life, a global, source-linked longevity intelligence index. We are inviting a small number of founding organisations to test a six-week monitoring pilot around five to ten questions that matter to their work. The pilot combines a baseline evidence and trial landscape, weekly meaningful-change briefs, university activity, and integrity or regulatory signals. I prepared a live exercise-research demonstration here: [private pilot URL]. If this addresses a real monitoring problem for [organisation], I would value a 20-minute conversation.

### Follow-up

> I am following up once because the pilot is deliberately small and may fit teams that cannot justify a full internal intelligence function. If [specific question] is not currently important, no response is needed. If it is, I can show the source-linked demonstration and define a six-week scope in one short call.

## Initial funnel

1. Build a shortlist of 15 organisations with a specific reason each might care.
2. Identify one decision-maker per organisation; do not mass-mail generic addresses.
3. Tailor the demonstration sentence to the recipient's programme or portfolio.
4. Aim for five substantive conversations.
5. Qualify two concrete pilot scopes.
6. Close one paid founding pilot.

The pilot page is intentionally unlisted and excluded from search indexing. Share it directly only with qualified prospective partners until the offer, delivery workflow, and source coverage have been validated through a real engagement.
