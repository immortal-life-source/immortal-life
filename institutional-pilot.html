What this demonstrates

A maintained evidence landscape, not a treatment recommendation. Record counts describe the indexed collection; registrations are not results; publication activity is not proof of efficacy. The pilot makes the evidence easier to inspect without manufacturing certainty.

Six-week structure

From a question to an operating intelligence habit.

  1. Week 0Define

    Select 5–10 topics, interventions, companies, competitors, or scientific questions and agree the decision context.

  2. Week 1Baseline

    Receive the opening evidence and trial landscape, source coverage statement, known gaps, and monitoring plan.

  3. Weeks 2–5Monitor

    Receive one professionally structured weekly brief plus direct links to the records behind every material change.

  4. Week 6Decide

    Review the closing report and a working session on what changed, what did not, and whether continued monitoring creates value.

Clear boundaries

Useful intelligence without pretending to be something it is not.

Source-linked. Material claims remain connected to the originating record so your team can inspect context and limitations.

Commercially controlled. Paid deliverables use only sources explicitly approved for paid distribution; directory-only sources remain outbound references.

Decision support. The service organises public research information. It does not diagnose, prescribe, recommend treatment, or replace scientific and legal diligence.

Coverage stated. Every pilot defines monitored sources, topics, dates, and known gaps instead of implying universal or perfectly complete coverage.
