What this tells a decision-maker

Exercise-related longevity research is active and growing. Human studies and registered trials exist, but the counts do not say that every intervention works. The value is seeing the meaningful changes, original sources, and important gaps in one concise view.

What happens during six weeks

You ask one question. We keep answering it as the evidence changes.

This is not a data dump. We agree what decision matters, create the starting picture, then report only changes worth your attention.

01

Starting answer

A clear, source-linked explanation of what is known now, what remains uncertain, and which evidence matters most.

02

Weekly answer

Trials that changed

Who is moving

Warnings and corrections

Relevant official notices, corrections, retractions, and withdrawals, stated without exaggeration.

06

Closing summary

What changed during the six weeks, what remains uncertain, and what is worth continuing to watch.

Six-week structure

A simple process from question to answer.

  1. Week 0Ask

    Choose one decision question and the 5–10 topics, products, organisations, or competitors that shape it.

  2. Week 1Understand

    Receive the starting answer: what is known, where the gaps are, and which sources we will watch.

  3. Weeks 2–5Stay informed

    Receive a short weekly explanation of meaningful changes, with direct links to the evidence behind each one.

  4. Week 6Decide

    Review what changed, what remained uncertain, and whether continued monitoring would help your team.

Clear boundaries

Useful intelligence without pretending to be something it is not.

Source-linked. Material claims remain connected to the originating record so your team can inspect context and limitations.

Commercially controlled. Paid deliverables use only sources explicitly approved for paid distribution; directory-only sources remain outbound references.

Decision support. The service organises public research information. It does not diagnose, prescribe, recommend treatment, or replace scientific and legal diligence.

Coverage stated. Every pilot defines monitored sources, topics, dates, and known gaps instead of implying universal or perfectly complete coverage.
